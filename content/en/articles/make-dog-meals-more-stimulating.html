  • stimulate them

Why these small changes make a real difference

In nature, a dog doesn't eat from a bowl.

It searches.
It explores.
It uses its nose, its head, its body.

When everything is served in seconds:

  • it doesn't get any mental exercise
  • it doesn't feel real satisfaction
  • it may keep asking for more

The result:

  • boredom
  • restlessness
  • meals swallowed too fast
  • less comfortable digestion

By slightly changing this moment…
you meet several needs at once.

1. The lick mat (instant calming effect)

If your dog eats too fast or seems restless, start here.

Spread a little wet food, plain unsweetened yogurt or a small portion of its ration on a lick mat for dogs.

Then watch.

It will slow down.
Focus.
Settle.

It's almost instant.

Licking has a natural calming effect.

2. The hidden meal (simple but powerful game)

This idea is often underestimated.

Instead of putting everything in the bowl, hide part of its food:

  • in a room
  • under an object
  • in an easy corner

Then let it search.

You're activating its natural instinct.

The result:

  • mental stimulation
  • energy spent
  • a more satisfied dog

And it takes less than a minute to set up.

3. The rolled-towel trick (simple and effective)

No accessory needed.

Take a towel.
Drop in a few kibbles.
Roll it up.

Your dog will have to:

  • sniff
  • unroll
  • think

It's simple… but very effective.

Ideal for:

  • rainy days
  • bored dogs
  • times when you want to slow things down

4. The enrichment toy (the smart version of mealtime)

Some dogs love to “work” for their food.

Fill a KONG-type enrichment toy with its food.

Then let it figure out how to get to its meal.

You turn:

a meal that lasts a few seconds
into an activity that lasts several minutes.

👉 You can also use a dog puzzle toy or an interactive treat dispenser.

Bonus: freeze the contents to make the activity last longer.

5. The slow feeder bowl (simple but effective)

If your dog swallows everything in seconds…

this small change can make a real difference.

A slow feeder bowl for dogs makes it:

  • slow down
  • work around obstacles
  • eat more calmly

Less air swallowed = more comfortable digestion.

6. Split the meal in two

A very simple idea… but very effective.

Give part of the meal:

  • in a game
  • as stimulation

Then the rest in the bowl.

Your dog:

  • gets stimulated
  • then settles down

A nice balance between activity and calm.

7. Create a calming mealtime routine

We don't always think about it…

But the atmosphere plays an important role.

A dog that eats in a calm setting:

  • digests better
  • feels safe
  • enjoys the moment more

You can simply:

  • avoid distractions
  • choose a consistent spot
  • keep a gentle routine

It becomes a reassuring landmark.

What you might notice (and it changes everything)

When meals are enriched, many dogs become:

  • calmer
  • less demanding
  • more satisfied
  • more focused

And sometimes even…
easier to live with.

These small adjustments, combined with simple tools like a lick mat or an interactive toy, can really make a difference.

Keep in mind

No need to do it all.

Choose:

  • one or two ideas
  • try them
  • watch the results

It's often the small changes that have the biggest impact.
