🐾 Contrary to popular belief, a cat never does something “just to annoy you”.

If it adopts a bothersome behaviour, it's almost always a way of communicating a need, discomfort or stress.

Scratching the couch, peeing outside the litter box, climbing everywhere or meowing nonstop…
These behaviours are common, and they always have a cause.

The good news is that they can be corrected effectively with the right actions.

In this article, you'll learn:

  • Why your cat acts this way
  • What to do in practice
  • How to prevent these behaviours day to day

The most common cat behaviours are linked to very specific needs

Here are the most common ones and how to respond.

It scratches the furniture

Couch, chairs, rugs… nothing is safe.
It's one of the most common cat behaviours.

Why?

  • To mark its territory
  • To maintain its claws (a natural need)
  • To release stress
  • Lack of stimulation

💡 What to do right away

  • Set up a suitable scratching post so it leaves the furniture alone
  • Place it near the targeted area (e.g. the couch)
  • Offer several options if needed
  • Reward it when it uses the post
  • Avoid scolding it (it doesn't work)

A well-placed scratching post can reduce this behaviour very quickly.

It goes outside the litter box

It's one of the most frustrating behaviours… but also one of the most revealing.

Why?

  • A dirty or poorly placed litter box
  • Stress or change (moving, a new pet…)
  • A health problem
  • Unsuitable litter

💡 What to do right away

  • Clean the litter box very regularly (ideally every day)
  • Check the location (quiet, accessible, stress-free)
  • Try a more suitable litter box to improve cleanliness
  • Add a second litter box if needed
  • See a veterinarian if the behaviour appears suddenly

To keep the area clean and stop litter from scattering around the box, a litter mat to limit the mess around the box can be very useful.

A clean, accessible and suitable litter box very often solves the problem.

It bites or attacks

Cats can bite or attack… sometimes without warning.
This behaviour is often misunderstood, but it's usually linked to play or discomfort.

Why?

  • Poorly channelled play (especially in young cats)
  • Overstimulation (petting for too long, too much handling)
  • Fear or discomfort
  • Not enough energy spent

💡 What to do right away

  • Avoid playing with your hands
  • Use suitable toys to channel its energy
  • Watch its signals (tail lashing, ears back…)
  • Stop the interaction at the first signs of irritation
  • Build in daily play sessions

Toys can also stimulate your cat mentally, like interactive games or food puzzles.

It meows excessively

A cat that meows a lot is often trying to say something.

Why?

  • Boredom or lack of stimulation
  • Need for attention
  • Stress or anxiety
  • Hunger or a disrupted routine

💡 What to do right away

  • Play with it every day (at least 5 to 15 minutes)
  • Establish a stable routine
  • Avoid responding to every meow

A cat that eats too fast can become restless or uncomfortable.

Using a slow feeder bowl to slow down its eating can help improve digestion and calm its behaviour.

It climbs everywhere

Tables, counters, cupboards… your cat loves to get up high.

Why?

  • A natural need for height and observation
  • A need for security
  • Curiosity
  • Boredom or lack of stimulation

💡 What to do right away

  • Set up a cat tree to meet its need to climb and observe
  • Create allowed areas
  • Make off-limits areas less attractive
  • Enrich its environment (games, stimulation)

Offering a suitable alternative is much more effective than forbidding.

Understanding your cat: the key to living together in harmony

A cat never acts “for no reason”.

Every behaviour is a signal:

  • An unmet need
  • Stress
  • Discomfort
  • A change in its environment

The more you observe your cat, the more effectively you can address the root of the problem.

When should you worry?

Here are the signs that should get your attention:

  • Sudden onset
  • Unusual intensity
  • A rapid change in behaviour
  • Loss of appetite or house-soiling

⚠️ In these cases, it's important to see a veterinarian.

Key takeaways

  • A cat doesn't do anything “to annoy you”
  • Every behaviour has a cause
  • Solutions come through environment and routine
  • Punishment doesn't work
  • Prevention is always more effective than correction

Observing and understanding your cat already solves a big part of the problem.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my cat always scratch in the same spot?

Because it's a strategic area for it: territory, scent and habit all play a role.

Should I punish my cat?

No. Punishment increases stress and often makes the problem worse.

How long does it take to correct a behaviour?

From a few days to a few weeks, depending on the cause and how consistent you are.

Improving your cat's behaviour day to day

🐾 To help your cat adopt better behaviours, a few things can improve the situation:

  • A suitable scratching post
  • Quality litter
  • Stimulating toys
  • A well-placed cat tree

These small adjustments can transform its behaviour… and make living together more pleasant.

According to specialists at VCA Animal Hospitals and the Cornell Feline Health Center, behaviours such as excessive meowing, litter box avoidance, restlessness or changes in habits can sometimes reveal a physical, emotional or environmental need in cats.
