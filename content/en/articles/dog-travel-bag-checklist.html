Vacations always take a bit of organizing.

You pack clothes, confirm reservations, prepare snacks for the road and everything you need to make the most of the trip.

Then comes the time to pack your dog's things.

Its food, leash, bowls and a few treats quickly find their way into a bag.

Yet some items easily slip under the radar when packing.

When you travel, your dog also leaves behind its routines, its environment and everything familiar to it.

It doesn't know where you're going.

It doesn't know how long the stay will last.

It simply discovers a new place, new smells and a different pace.

That's why packing your dog's travel bag isn't just about gathering what it needs to eat, drink or go for walks.

It's also a way to make the transition more pleasant and leave with peace of mind.

Before you zip it up, here's what deserves a spot in its bag.

The information you hope you'll never need

Before leaving, take a few minutes to gather important information about your dog.

Keep in a pouch or on your phone:

  • A recent photo saved on your phone
  • Its microchip number
  • Its licence tag number
  • Your veterinary clinic's contact information
  • Its list of medications, if needed

This information takes up little space, but it's much easier to find when it's all in one place.

Things that remind it of home

When you arrive somewhere new, your dog discovers an environment completely different from the one it knows.

Recognizing a few familiar objects can help it adapt more easily. They provide reassuring landmarks during this transition.

Consider bringing:

  • Its usual cushion or mattress
  • Its favourite blanket
  • A toy it uses regularly
  • Its mat or resting bed

💡 If you can only choose one item, go for the one your dog spends the most time with at home.

What saves you last-minute shopping

Finding a pet store or grocery store nearby isn't always as easy as you'd think, especially after several hours on the road.

Before leaving, make sure you have:

  • Enough food for the entire stay
  • A few extra portions in case of delays or a change of plans
  • Its usual treats
  • An airtight container to keep its food fresh
  • Its bowls or a collapsible bowl for travel

💡 Changing your dog's food during vacation can complicate an otherwise great trip. Packing a few extra portions can save you a last-minute search at your destination.

If you travel often with your dog, a travel bag designed to carry its food, bowls, toys and accessories can simplify packing.

The little items that sometimes save the day

These items take up little space in the bag, but one situation is all it takes to be glad you brought them.

Here are a few accessories that deserve a place in your dog's travel bag:

  • A spare leash
  • Poop bags
  • A microfibre towel
  • A tick remover
  • A paw pad balm
  • Hypoallergenic wipes made for dogs

💡 A walk in the rain, a hike in the woods or an impromptu swim is sometimes all it takes to put these accessories to good use.

Before hitting the road

The trip doesn't start at your destination.

For your dog, the adventure begins as soon as the car starts.

A few checks before leaving can make the drive more pleasant for everyone.

Consider bringing:

  • A bottle of water and a travel bowl
  • A seat belt or harness designed for the car
  • A waterproof blanket or towel to protect the seat
  • Your destination address saved on your phone
  • A compact flashlight if you're planning camping or evening activities

💡 A flashlight takes up very little space and can come in handy for a night outing, a power outage or a late walk.

💡 If the drive is long, plan a few stops so your dog can stretch its legs, drink water and relieve itself.

💡 If your dog drools a lot, whines or vomits during car rides, talk to your veterinarian before the holidays. A few adjustments can make the trip more comfortable for it.

🧡 Before leaving, check:

☐ Up-to-date ID tag

☐ Recent photo of your dog saved on your phone

☐ Food for the whole stay plus a few extra portions

☐ Water and travel bowl

☐ Its usual cushion, mat or blanket

☐ Spare leash

☐ Veterinary contact information handy

The 30-second test before closing the bag

Before leaving, take a few seconds for one last look at what you've packed.

Simply ask yourself:

If my dog had to spend a whole day in a place it doesn't know, have I thought of everything that could keep it comfortable, safe and well cared for?

If the answer is yes, chances are you've covered the essentials.

The best travel bag isn't the one with the most accessories.

It's the one that helps you avoid forgetting things and lets your dog fully enjoy the adventure by your side.
