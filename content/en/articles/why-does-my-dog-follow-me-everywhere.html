For a dog, movement around the house is full of information.

A door opening.

Shoes going on.

A leash being picked up.

A cupboard moving.

All these little details can signal something interesting.

That's why it notices your movements before you've even left the room.

It doesn't automatically mean it lacks independence.

It simply likes to keep an eye on what's going on in the house.

Why has your dog become clingier than before?

If you've noticed a recent change, take a few minutes to think about what has happened over the past few months.

A move.

A new baby.

A new pet.

A long vacation.

A schedule change.

All these changes can explain why it's seeking your company more.

If your dog rarely followed you before and this habit appeared recently, take a moment to think about what has changed around it. The answer is sometimes where you least expect it.

When you're not feeling well

Many people notice their dog becomes more present during a period of fatigue or illness.

It may choose to sleep closer to you or follow you around the house more often.

After several days apart

After a trip away or a vacation, it's not unusual for a dog to seek more contact for a while.

This reaction tends to fade after a few days.

After a move

New sounds.

New places to explore.

During this adjustment period, it may seek your presence more.

When a newcomer arrives in the home

During a major change in your schedule

Remote work, retirement or a new job can transform everyone's routines.

Your dog adjusts its own too.

Age can also play a role

In puppies

In adult dogs

🐾 Do you recognize your dog in several of the situations above?

In most cases, following its people from room to room is a perfectly normal habit for a dog.

It's not necessarily a sign of anxiety or a problem to fix.

Is my dog following me in a normal way?

Ask yourself these few questions:

✔ Can it sleep in another room without difficulty?

✔ Does it stay calm when you close a door?

✔ Can it relax on its own for a while?

✔ Does it stay calm when you leave the house?

✔ Does it settle down quickly after you leave?

If you answered yes to most of these questions, it's probably a perfectly normal situation.

When should you pay closer attention?

Following someone from room to room is generally nothing to worry about.

However, it can be useful to look further when you notice:

  • frequent whining;
  • restlessness as soon as a door closes;
  • destruction while you're away;
  • an inability to calm down alone;
  • marked stress when it loses track of you.

In that case, advice from a veterinarian or a canine behaviour specialist can be helpful.
